# Presentation editing

- Prefer ASCII in presentation slides, speaker notes, labels, and related documentation. Use straight apostrophes and quotes, ordinary hyphens, and simple ASCII separators and arrows. Use non-ASCII only when needed for accurate names or mathematical meaning, and check edited text for accidental smart punctuation.
- Keep speaker notes closely aligned with the text and figures visible on the current slide, including the current reveal step. Follow the order of the material on screen so listeners can connect what they hear with what they see.
- Elaborate on visible ideas with substantive reasoning and concrete examples. For example, "time and money" can be explained through learning and using a tool, interpreting changing results, and building and running the system. The notes should help explain the slide rather than simply repeat it.
- Discuss specific numbers, workflow descriptions, and figure details while their supporting content is visible. Avoid making listeners reconstruct another slide to follow the current explanation. If a detail is essential here, provide a concise visual anchor for it.
- On overview and transition slides, name the visible question, explain why it follows from the preceding question, and establish what the next section will resolve. Allow enough speaking time for that connection to land.
- Before finishing a notes edit, compare the spoken text with the slide at each reveal step. Preserve useful detail while correcting references that arrive before their visual support or rely on material no longer on screen.
